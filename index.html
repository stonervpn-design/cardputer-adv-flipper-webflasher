Flipper — Cardputer ADV

Public build · app image · ESP32-S3 · needs a microSD card

Your browser can’t flash directly. Use Chrome or Edge on a desktop, or see the manual method below. Serial access was blocked. Reload the page over https:// and allow the serial permission.
Before you click: plug the Cardputer into your computer with a USB‑C data cable (not charge‑only). Choose Install when prompted — it does a clean flash of the whole chip.

Steps

  1. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer (phones and Safari/Firefox can’t flash).
  2. Connect the Cardputer via USB‑C.
  3. Click Connect & Install and pick the device’s serial port.
  4. Choose Install, confirm the erase, and wait for it to finish.
  5. Press the RESET button (side, next to the USB‑C port) once when done — the firmware boots.
Insert a microSD card. Unlike the M5Stick build, the Cardputer stores its databases on a real microSD card — not internal flash. For SubGHz KeeLoq decoding, NFC dictionaries, and IR universal remotes to work, download the SD files (see SD card files below) and extract them to the root of a FAT32 microSD, then insert it. You end up with /subghz, /nfc, /infrared, and /Manifest on the card.

If no serial port appears

The S3 sometimes needs to be put in download mode by hand:

  • Hold G0 (the button on top of the Cardputer), tap RESET, then release G0.
  • Click Connect & Install again and select the port.
  • After flashing, press RESET once to boot.

Manual flashing (no browser support)

With Python installed:

  • pip install esptool
  • esptool --chip esp32s3 --port COMx --baud 921600 write_flash 0x0 Flipper-cardputer_adv-public-merged.bin

Replace COMx with your port (macOS/Linux: /dev/tty.usbmodem… or /dev/ttyACM0). Flash mode/size/freq are already baked into the image. Don’t forget the microSD step above.

SD card files

The Cardputer reads its databases from the microSD card. Download the pack below and extract the files to the root of a FAT32 microSD, so the card looks exactly like this:

16 files, all public data — SubGHz KeeLoq (gate/garage) keys, NFC dictionaries, and IR universal‑remote databases. No card (or missing files) means SubGHz decoding, NFC lookups, and IR remotes won’t work. Re‑flashing the firmware does not touch the card.
